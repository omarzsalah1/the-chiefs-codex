### K1-01 · L55 (post-Brancaleoni italic reflection) · Cat 1 (scene→thought) — COMPRESSION
**Excerpt:** "*I learned something today… I did not threaten him with my anger—I threatened him with Federico's. The gatekeeper must be a lion… and a fox… But the lion's roar is borrowed… Without that echo, I am nothing.*"
**Why:** Triple-states one insight the journal lands better at L109–111 (roar → cloak → reflected light). Worse: the lion/fox formula here pre-echoes Federico's lion/fox teaching at L93 — Bertoldo "discovers" the doctrine an hour before the Duke teaches it to him, deflating the appointment scene. And it has Bertoldo explaining his own maneuver's cleverness — the Codex Coup Rule violation type.
**Direction:** Cut the reflection's second half (from "When I spoke…" through "…back down."), keep the somatic fear-data ("shaking so badly he had to grip the desk") and at most one short un-clever beat of not-understanding-yet. Let Federico introduce lion/fox; let the journal land the insight once.
**Checks:** Dial ✓ (reduces commentary) · Coup Rule ✓ (repairs a violation) · no new aphorism.
**Recommendation: Strong yes.**

### K1-02 · L103–113 (first journal entry) · Cat 3 (aphoristic opportunity) — THE CHAPTER'S 1-APHORISM SLOT
**Excerpt:** "*1461. First Day at the Door. Fifty-three petitioners. One threat. The door held.*"
**Why:** The title — the Art of Subtraction — is never earned in-chapter; the chapter dramatizes petitioners-as-theft but never turns the inversion (the gatekeeper serves by removing, not providing). The journal, in a notary-son's ledger idiom, is the native place: count what was taken away. The theft-vehicle is spent in-chapter (L27, L89); the new vehicle must be arithmetic/ledger, not theft.
**Direction:** One new sentence inside the entry, notary register, earning "subtraction" — the day tallied as removals (what was kept out, not what was given). No thesis, no "I understood that…" framing.
**Checks:** Dial ✓ (journal = archival organ) · earned-by-scene ✓ (fifty-three turned away, one threat) · consumes the 1/chapter aphorism cap.
**Recommendation: Strong yes.**

### K1-03 · L117–121 (kicker) · Cat 2 (irony) — SHARPEN, NON-APHORISTIC
**Excerpt:** "None of them understood that something had changed. The door… had acquired a guardian who understood its true nature… And for the first time that day… Duke Federico da Montefeltro slept soundly."
**Why:** "None of them understood… true nature" explains the kicker before it lands (thesis-at-the-beat, anti-pattern). The stronger irony is already latent in the sleep symmetry: the Duke sleeps soundly because another man has taken up the not-sleeping. The cost-transfer is the book's whole tragedy in miniature — render it, don't announce it.
**Direction:** Trim the explanatory clause(s); let the fifty-three beds / the Duke's sound sleep / the man still at the desk carry the exchange unnamed. Non-aphoristic phrasing (K1-02 holds the aphorism slot).
**Checks:** Dial ✓ (kicker is fingerprint-native) · no new aphorism if phrased as image, not maxim.
**Recommendation: Maybe.**

### K1-04 · L103 (the cipher clause) · Cat 4 (editorial/archival) — FRAME WHISPER
**Excerpt:** "using the shorthand cipher he'd learned from his father's notary work, he wrote his first entry"

**Checks:** Dial: borderline (in-scene archival) · no new aphorism if kept as clause.
**Recommendation: Maybe.**
